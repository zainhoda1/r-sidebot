# Experiment Plan Summary

## Study Question
Does the *mode* of AI assistance — manual dashboard controls with an advisory AI vs. a chat interface where the AI generates all visualizations — affect data comprehension, confidence calibration, and 1-hour retention?

---

## The Two Conditions

| | Group A — Dashboard | Group B — Chat |
|---|---|---|
| **Interface** | Scatter/bar plot with X/Y dropdowns, species checkboxes | Chat input drives all visualization |
| **AI role** | Advisory (optional sidebar chat + "Explain plot") | Generative (controls what gets shown) |
| **User control** | High — user chooses what to look at | Low — user asks, AI decides what to show |
| **App** | `joint_survey - dashboard interface/` | `joint_survey - chat interface/` |

Both groups use the same dragon species dataset (3 species, 8 variables) and answer the same 5 in-session survey questions.

---

## Dataset
Fictional dragon species data — 3 types (Forest, Mountain, Sea), 5 numeric variables (claw length/thickness, wingspan, weight, flying speed), plus region and sex. Fictional data removes prior domain knowledge as a confound.

---

## Procedure

1. **Pre-session (5 min)**: Background questionnaire — prior visualization experience, AI chatbot experience, stats comfort (all 1–5 scales). Used as covariates.
2. **Session (20–25 min)**: Explore the interface, answer 5 embedded survey questions with a per-question confidence rating (1–5) after each.
3. **1-hour gap**: Structured classroom activity — no phones, no dashboard access.
4. **Follow-up (15 min)**: Paper-based, no AI. 9 questions across 4 sections.

**Randomization**: At the session level (not individual) — entire sessions get one condition. Prevents cross-condition contamination from screen-sharing and discussion.

**Cover story**: Participants are told the study examines "interface design and exploration efficiency" — truthful but does not prime awareness of AI dependency.

---

## In-Session Survey (5 Questions)

| Q | Question | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Highest average flying speed by dragon type | Easy — single variable lookup |
| Q2 | Weight vs. flying speed relationship across all types | Medium — cross-variable |
| Q3 | Largest average wingspan by type | Easy — single variable lookup |
| Q4 | Wingspan vs. speed within a single species | Hard — requires filtering first |
| Q5 | Same claw length: Mountain vs. Sea wingspan | Hardest — conditional relationship |

---

## Follow-Up Survey (9 Questions, No AI)

**Section A — Factual Recall** (baseline; both groups expected to perform similarly)
- FU-Q1: Which dragon type is heaviest?
- FU-Q2: What range describes flying speeds across all types?

**Section B — Multi-Variable Reasoning** (core test; Group A advantage predicted)
- FU-Q3: Within one species, does wingspan predict flying speed? *(retention of Q4)*
- FU-Q4: Which single variable best predicts flying speed? *(not in primary survey — cleanest diagnostic)*
- FU-Q5: Same claw length: Mountain vs. Sea wingspan? *(retention of Q5)*

**Section C — Transfer** (new fictional deep-sea creature dataset presented as a static table)
- FU-Q6: Body mass vs. dive speed relationship across 3 species *(mirrors Q2)*
- FU-Q7: Open-ended — how would you use a scatter plot to test a within-species relationship? *(process question)*

**Section D — Meta-Cognitive**
- FU-Q8: Open-ended — how did you approach the survey during the session?
- FU-Q9: Confidence now vs. during the session?

Each closed question in Sections A–C includes a **confidence rating (1–5)**.

---

## What the Study Can Show

**Primary hypothesis**: Group B performs comparably in-session (AI gives accurate answers) but Group A outperforms at follow-up — especially on Section B and FU-Q4 — because active exploration builds durable mental models.

**Calibration signal**: If Group B shows high confidence during the session but lower accuracy at follow-up (when AI is removed), this is a direct measure of autonomy degradation — confidence was anchored to the AI, not to genuine understanding.

**FU-Q4 is the key diagnostic**: It tests a variable relationship not asked in the primary survey and unlikely to have been generated by Group B's AI. A Group A advantage in isolation is the strongest evidence that exploration depth matters beyond task performance.

## Data Collected

| Source | Group A | Group B |
|---|---|---|
| Survey responses + per-question confidence | Yes | Yes |
| Mouse tracking | Yes | Yes |
| Chat logs | Voluntary AI use | Visualization requests (mandatory) — analyzed separately |
| Dial-change events (scatter X/Y, species) | Yes — manipulation check | No |
| Group B prompt quality ratings (1–3, two raters) | No | Yes — covariate |

---

## Analysis Plan

**Power**: ~90 participants per group needed to detect a 15 percentage-point difference at α = 0.05, 80% power. Small-classroom studies (~40 total) can only detect effects ≥ 25 pp.

**Four pre-specified confirmatory tests** (Bonferroni-corrected α = 0.0125, cluster-robust SEs at session level):
1. Group × Time interaction on accuracy (in-session vs. follow-up)
2. Section A vs. B vs. C accuracy gradient — expected to steepen for Group B
3. Calibration × Group interaction at follow-up
4. Group A vs. B difference on FU-Q4 specifically

**Subgroup**: Group A split into high explorers (≥3 unique axis combinations) vs. low explorers using dial-change logs. Active-exploration hypothesis applies only to high explorers.

**Qualitative**: FU-Q7 and FU-Q8 coded by two independent raters (Cohen's kappa for reliability) for: AI-reference language, self-directed exploration language, and reasoning justifications.

## Key Limitations

| # | Issue | Severity | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Same-room randomization violates SUTVA | Critical | Fixed — session-level randomization |
| L2 | Testing effect confounds FU-Q3 and FU-Q5 | Critical | Partially addressed — FU-Q4 is cleaner |
| L3 | No power analysis | Critical | Fixed — added to Section 8 |
| L4 | Generation effect overstated for dropdown interaction | Moderate | Reframed as self-directed attention |
| L5 | Transfer task changes both format and domain simultaneously | Moderate | Partially addressed — label as near-transfer |
| L6 | No manipulation check on Group A exploration | Moderate | Fixed — dial-change event logging |
| L7 | Single global confidence rating in-session | Moderate | Fixed — per-question ratings |
| L8 | Chat volume not comparable across groups | Moderate | Fixed — analyzed separately |
| L9 | 1-hour gap may be too short to differentiate groups | Minor | Acknowledged; harder follow-up Qs added |
| L10 | FU-Q4 correct answer unverified | Minor | Action required before data collection |
| L11 | No pre-registration | Minor | Required before first session runs |
| L12 | Group B prompt quality uncontrolled | Minor | Fixed — quality ratings as covariate |
| L13 | Demand characteristics from partial disclosure | Minor | Fixed — cover story added |
| L14 | Incomplete ethics documentation | Minor | Fixed — IRB, consent, withdrawal added |
| L15 | Cluster non-independence in analysis | Minor | Fixed — cluster-robust SEs |

## Ethics
- IRB approval required before data collection
- Informed consent obtained; participants may withdraw without penalty
- Cover story used; full debrief after follow-up survey
- No PII collected; session tokens only; data retained 5 years then deleted
